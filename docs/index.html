Why your own server

Every VPN you've been sold has one thing to block. Yours has none.

Commercial VPNs share an IP among thousands of strangers. When one address gets flagged, all of them go dark, and the app-store listing goes with it. That's not a bug in a particular app — it's the shape of the business.

A server of your own is one IP, used by the people you know, behind a domain nobody has a reason to look at. There's no company to pressure, no listing to remove, no subscription to cancel. If the address is ever banned, you get a new server in fifteen minutes and nobody at home has to change anything.

And it costs what a server costs — about $6 a month — not $6 a month per person.

CollectedByte counters and last-connection time per device. That's all the data there is.
Not collectedSites, DNS queries, destinations, content. Access logs are off on both machines.
Who holds keysYou. Generated on your machine, stored in a file you keep. Burrow has no backend to hold anything.
If it's bannedNew server, same domain, ~15 minutes. Devices at home reconnect without being touched. (With the two-server layout.)
Honest limitsNetworks with strict allow-lists can block anything foreign, and no protocol fixes that. Burrow tells you when it can't help.
