Why your own server

Every VPN you've been sold is someone else's server. This one is yours.

Commercial VPNs share an IP among thousands of strangers, so you inherit their reputation: banks, shops and streaming services flag the address as "VPN" and you get captchas, refused logins and toggling the app on and off. That's not a bug in a particular app — it's the shape of the business.

A server of your own is one IP, used by the people you know. Nobody in the middle sees your traffic, nobody can sell it, and nobody can change the terms. And it costs what a server costs — about $6 a month — not $6 a month per person.

One honest limit: some carrier and corporate networks restrict VPN traffic altogether, and no setup on your side changes that. Burrow tells you when the network, not the setup, is the problem.

Split tunnelingBanking and other apps that refuse VPN connections keep working, no toggling. Local services go direct, the rest through the tunnel.
PrivateYour own server, so nobody sees your traffic. Access logs are off, and the only numbers kept — bytes and last-seen, per device — live on your server, nowhere else. Privacy, stated plainly.
FastNo shared bandwidth and no queue of strangers on the same address: the server's whole pipe belongs to the handful of devices you added.
Whose machineNothing runs on anyone else's infrastructure. The server is rented in your name and stays yours — Burrow runs no servers at all.

Provider won't take your card?

It happens — some hosts reject prepaid or debit cards, or a card from a bank they don't know. You can still rent a server. Three ways round it, in the order I'd try them.

01 Someone else pays for you

A friend or relative whose card the provider accepts rents the server. It's about $6 a month, or they just keep paying it — it's the price of a coffee.

02 Hosts that take crypto or local payment methods

Plenty of providers accept PayPal, crypto or local payment methods. The guide lists which ones are known to work and what to watch for.

See the list in the guide

03 Virtual-card services

They work — and they're last on this list for a reason.

Fees, identity checks, and they come and go. We don't partner with any of them or take a cut — check one yourself before trusting it with a card.
